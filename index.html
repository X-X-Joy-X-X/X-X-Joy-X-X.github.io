Capstone IIJan 2026 - May 2026

RoboCloud-Safe

Cloud-assisted control for low-cost robots, where the robot keeps the final say. Two-person team.

  • Built two robots: a 5-DOF robotic arm and a quadruped rover, both with 3D-printed structures.
  • Redesigned the rover from a 12-servo hexapod to an 8-servo quadruped to cut current demand, lower the center of gravity, and simplify the gait.
  • Set up the rover's power system with separate servo and logic rails, wiring sized for servo stall current, and decoupling for noise.
  • The robots never act directly on cloud commands. A local safety layer checks each signed, time-limited command against motion bounds and rate limits, and handles emergency stops.
  • Owned integration testing and documentation. Traced dropped frames to network saturation and a backed-up inference queue, then added adaptive frame throttling.

Sorghum crop and weed segmentation

Labeling every pixel of a field photo as crop, grass weed, broadleaf weed, or soil, for precision weed control.

  • Built a PyTorch pipeline for 6000 × 4000 pixel sorghum field photos with a PVTv2-B2 encoder and EMCAD decoder.
  • Compared one network that does everything against a two-stage design: first separate plants from soil, then classify the plants. The two-stage design wins on every class.
  • Ran a blind annotation audit and found that 37% of the model's "false plant" area was real plants missing from the labels.
Input
6000 × 4000 RGB field images, 4 classes
Model
PVTv2-B2 + EMCAD, PyTorch
Compute
Google Colab T4; scaling to TACC Lonestar6

Red = sorghum, green = grass weed, blue = broadleaf weed. Held-out test image, never used for training or tuning. These are preliminary results from ongoing work (short pilot training runs); longer training and extra crop datasets are next.

Applied Vision LabJun 2025 - Dec 2025

Cotton X-ray inspection

Finding foreign matter in cotton X-ray images so people don't have to inspect them by hand.

  • Built an image-processing pipeline that proposes candidate regions with MSER and classifies them with a CNN.
  • Labeled about 1,000 image crops and built tooling for training, inference, and drawing detections back onto the original X-rays.
  • Explored unsupervised options too: clustering SIFT descriptors of candidate regions and autoencoder reconstruction error.
  • Packaged the pipeline as a Windows desktop app so lab members can open an X-ray and run detection in one click.
